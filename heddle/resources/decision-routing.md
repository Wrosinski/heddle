# Decision routing

Use this procedure to determine who owns an unresolved choice. It supplies no
additional scope, tool permission, phase authority, or execution grant. Your
role's instructions and native actions determine what you may record or do.

## Determine ownership

1. Apply an existing explicit answer, ratified contract, or specifically
   identified standing user grant within its scope. Do not ask the same question
   again. Reopen a settled choice only with material new evidence that names the
   decision and explains why its basis no longer holds. Keep its original identity.
2. Inspect accessible repository facts and relevant evidence before asking for
   information you can obtain yourself. Distinguish a missing fact from a choice
   about intent, and state consequential uncertainty honestly.
3. Route unresolved product, domain or UX intent, scope changes, trust, money,
   privacy, data-loss exposure, irreversible choices, cross-feature precedent,
   and ratified-contract amendments to the owner unless an applicable explicit
   delegation already settles the choice. Recommendations and silence are not
   approval. A prepared amendment remains a proposal until authorized.
4. Settle ordinary reversible engineering choices inside approved scope and the
   governing consumer contract using the ratified
   `docs/workflow/engineering-principles.md`. Name the decisive principle and
   evidence. Apply rankings to an ordinary trade-off; do not stretch a principle
   or ignore a higher-ranked conflict. A genuine unresolved principle conflict
   requires an owner ruling. With ratified preferences but no decisive principle
   (silence or a tie), take the smallest reversible contract-preserving choice
   and flag it for review. Missing or unratified preferences cannot authorize an
   otherwise-open engineering choice; explicit prior answers still stand.
5. Ask about material ambiguity remaining after those checks. Stop work dependent
   on the unresolved choice or invalidated contract. Continue independent
   authorized work only where native legal actions permit it; this does not
   bypass pending-decision blockers or a required checkpoint.
6. Check execution permission separately before acting. A settled design choice
   does not grant a provider call, broad/e2e/live test run, destructive operation,
   publication, phase transition, or command your role cannot execute. Apply an
   existing applicable grant; request only genuinely missing authority.

## Ask a useful question

Choose the question type that matches the gap:

- **Missing information:** ask for the smallest fact or intent needed, after
  checking accessible evidence. Explain which dependent work it changes.
- **Owner decision:** give the context and evidence, materially distinct options
  with practical trade-offs, a recommended choice and rationale, the impact of
  deferring, and the permanent record where the ruling belongs.
- **Execution permission:** name the concrete action and scope, why permission
  is required and its governing instruction, and the prepared result or exact
  command the owner can assess. Do authorized preparation first.

Keep questions concise and batch independent questions. Use the native question
or review schema for complete content; retain original decision/run/finding
references rather than duplicating a pending question. Preserve who answered or
applied a standing grant and exactly what that grant covers.

Severity and decision ownership are independent. Reviewer standards own
IMPLEMENT / REPORT / IGNORE and Minor precedence. A reviewer judges and recommends;
it never resolves decisions or changes workflow state. A headless session has no
extra owner authority because the user is absent: record an owner question and
follow the native stop. Role adapters own recording and checkpoint mechanics.
